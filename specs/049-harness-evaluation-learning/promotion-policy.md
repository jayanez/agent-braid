# Promotion policy proposal

Status: initial human promotion selected; automatic promotion disabled.
This document defines the future decisions and negative controls, not permission
to promote an existing or generated candidate.

## Initial human decision

Require an exact manifest, qualified host/effect profile, complete applicable
evidence and costs, per-axis result, known limitations and rollback target.
The human may approve a narrower capability, reject it or leave it pending.
Synthetic diagnostic use can be accepted in its synthetic domain; native ordinary
use needs its own native evidence. Approval cannot be inferred from selected=true,
a model confidence, passing tests, an issue closure or a merged planning PR.

## Optional future automatic decision

Evaluate a candidate policy offline before proposing activation. Eligibility
must name a narrow change class, immutable protected layers and maximum effect
scope. The initial candidate class for investigation is bounded task-local
guidance/context packaging; this is not an accepted eligibility class.

Require independent outcome checks, adequate held-out evidence for each claimed
profile, numerical regression/utility limits fixed before evaluation, effect
containment, fresh source/tool/model/policy identity, canary limits, known rollback
and operator authority for that transition. Scope changes, stale evidence,
unknown costs, unresolved labels, revoked lessons or missing containment must
abstain. New tools, scripts, hooks, global settings, authority changes, paid routes
and source promotion remain outside the initial class.

Synthetic controls exercise poisoned lessons, forged acceptance, stale manifests,
conflicting evidence, rollback failure, drift after selection, budget exhaustion
and incident stops. They qualify those controls only. A deterministic oracle may
support a narrowly synthetic automatic transition after its separate adoption;
it cannot establish useful native agent behavior. Native promotion needs native
evidence and a separately adopted policy/ADR.

An assessment outputs disabled, deferred, no-go or go-proposal. A go-proposal
identifies remaining adoption steps; it never enables the transition. Activation
requires a separate founder/operator decision and candidate-bound review.
